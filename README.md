# Implied Open

A single web page that estimates where the S&P 500, Nasdaq-100, Nasdaq Composite, Dow, Russell 2000 and S&P/TSX Composite will open at 9:30 a.m. Eastern, from overnight futures. It has no inputs, fetches prices when opened and refreshes every minute. Each result comes with a plain-language explanation.

## Layout
- `site/index.html`: the page.
- `site/robots.txt`, `site/_headers`: keep search engines out. The page also carries a `noindex` meta tag.
- `supabase/functions/implied-open/index.ts`: the data relay.
- `site/accuracy.html`: the track-record page (https://implied-open.soesraider.com/accuracy).
- `supabase/functions/implied-open-accuracy/index.ts` + `supabase/accuracy.sql`: the accuracy logger and its database setup.
- `private/`: Matt's login-protected version (https://implied-open-pvt.soesraider.com), a separate Cloudflare Pages project. It builds from `site/` and adds a TSX estimate from Montreal Exchange SXF futures. See HANDOFF.md, "Private SXF site".

## Where the data comes from
Browsers can't read Yahoo Finance directly, so the page calls a small Supabase Edge Function:
`https://awagbieeouxxhytdhloi.supabase.co/functions/v1/implied-open`
The function fetches fixed symbols from Yahoo's free, unofficial chart feed. Index closes: ^GSPC, ^NDX, ^IXIC, ^DJI, ^RUT, ^GSPTSE (daily bars, plus the 9:30 one-minute bar after the open, for the 9:31 level). Futures: one specific quarterly contract each of E-mini S&P 500, E-mini Nasdaq-100, Mini Dow and E-mini Russell 2000, chosen by date (e.g. ESZ26.CME, NQZ26.CME, YMZ26.CBT, RTYZ26.CME; month codes H=Mar, M=Jun, U=Sep, Z=Dec). It switches to the next contract from the Monday of expiry week (contracts expire on the third Friday of March, June, September and December). If Yahoo ever stops serving the specific contract, it falls back to the continuous series (ES=F, NQ=F, YM=F, RTY=F). Canada drivers: CL=F (oil), NG=F (natural gas), GC=F (gold), CADUSD=X. It also pulls the Government of Canada 10-year benchmark yield (series BD.CDN.10YR.DQ.YLD, daily) from the Bank of Canada Valet API. Overnight context row (background only, not used in any estimate): ^VIX, ^TNX, ZN=F, DX-Y.NYB, ^STOXX50E, ^GDAXI, ^FTSE, BZ=F, CL=F, HG=F, fetched together in one Yahoo "spark" request (5-minute bars, 5 days) and cached for a minute; change is measured from the price at the last US cash close (Europe: from its official previous close). WCS: monthly WCS and WTI averages from the Government of Alberta Economic Dashboard API (`api.economicdata.alberta.ca`, table OilPrices), cached 6 hours; terms allow non-commercial reuse with the Government of Alberta named as the source. It's read-only, needs no key, can't fetch anything else and doesn't touch the database.

## The calculation
- Implied open = last cash close + (futures now − futures at that close). Using the futures' point move builds in the normal gap between futures and cash prices.
- "Futures at that close" is normally Yahoo's daily futures close, which equals the futures price at the 4 p.m. cash close. Two exceptions, where the relay takes the price from 5-minute futures bars instead: after a 1 p.m. early close (futures trade on until 1:15 p.m. and Yahoo's daily close is that 1:15 price, so the relay uses the 1:00 p.m. price), and for the TSX after a US-only holiday (Yahoo shows no futures trading on US holidays, so it uses the last futures price before the TSX's close).
- Nasdaq Composite: there are no futures, so it applies the NQ percentage move to the Composite close.
- S&P/TSX Composite: it applies the ES (S&P 500) percentage move, because Canadian index futures (SXF on the Montreal Exchange) have no free feed. The exchange's terms forbid retrieving and republishing its data without permission. A "Canada drivers" panel adds oil, natural gas, gold, the Canadian dollar and the 10-year yield, with plain-language notes. A move counts as meaningful only above oil 0.5%, gas 0.75%, gold 0.3% or CAD 0.1%.
- After the open, the page shows each index's level at 9:31 a.m. (the close of the 9:30 one-minute bar, the first full minute of trading) instead of the estimate. The first printed open is partly built from the previous day's prices (many stocks haven't traded at 9:30:00), so it understates the real move. From 9:30 until that minute is over (about 9:31:10; Yahoo's index prices are only seconds behind) the card says "Opening now" and keeps the last estimate. If Yahoo has no 9:31 level, it falls back to the first printed open and says so.
- Market holidays and 1 p.m. early closes: fixed lists for 2026-2027, checked against the official NYSE and TSX calendars, are in the page, the relay and the logger (keep the three copies the same; extend them before 2028). On a holiday, or the evening before one, the page estimates the next real open; the TSX card follows the TSX calendar (e.g. open on US Thanksgiving, closed on Boxing Day).
- If Yahoo stops serving the specific futures contract, the page shows a yellow "Data problem" note while it uses the continuous series. It retries every minute and the note clears by itself; if it's still there a day later, the code needs fixing.
- Move-size words on the cards ("a small move", "a large move" etc.) are set per index from its own history: the 30th, 80th and 98th percentiles of its absolute daily closing move over five years (Nov 2021 - Nov 2026), so "large" means bigger than 4 days in 5 and "very large" about 5 days a year for every index. The cut-offs are in `SIZE` in `site/index.html` (TSX copy in `private/sxf.js`); refresh them about once a year.
- Estimates get more reliable closer to 9:30 a.m. ET: overnight trading is light, more traders join after about 8 a.m. and economic data often comes out at 8:30 a.m.

## Caveats
Yahoo futures prices are about 10 minutes delayed (index prices only seconds). The feed is unofficial and may change. The holiday lists end with 2027.

## Accuracy tracking
- Every 5 minutes, a scheduled job in the Supabase database (pg_cron job `implied-open-accuracy-tick`) calls the Edge Function `implied-open-accuracy`. From 6 p.m. to 9:30 a.m. Eastern it saves the implied open for each index, using the page's formula, filed under the futures price's time (Yahoo's futures run about 10 minutes behind). Holidays are skipped (it uses the same calendars as the page). On weekdays from 9:40 to 10:30 a.m. and at 4:30 p.m. it saves the actual official open, the index level at 9:31 a.m. (from 1-minute bars) and the level at 9:35 a.m.
- The accuracy page compares with the level at 9:31 by default for the US indexes and the level at 9:35 for the TSX (which opens more slowly); the official open is the third option. Yahoo keeps 1-minute data for only about 30 days, so days before Oct 6, 2026 have no 9:31 level and use 9:35 instead (the page says how many).
- Data lives in the database schema `implied_open` (tables `snapshots` and `actuals`), which isn't exposed through Supabase's API. The function reads it over a direct database connection. Its GET returns a JSON summary for the page with no key needed. Its POST needs a shared secret stored in Supabase Vault (`implied_open_log_secret`).
- History from Aug 26 to Nov 4, 2026 was rebuilt from Yahoo's 5-minute futures history (marked "rebuilt" on the page). Live snapshots start Nov 4, 2026.
- Futures contract-switch days (Tuesday of a quarterly expiry week through the following Monday) are flagged (grey on the day-by-day chart) and, since the fix below, counted in the averages by default (a checkbox leaves them out). Yahoo's intraday and daily futures series switch to the next contract on different days, which made September 2026's estimates about 1% too high on those days. Fix (2026-11-04): the page and the logger now use one specific contract for both the live price and the reference close, so the two can't come from different contracts. Replaying Sep 14–21, 2026 with the fix, the S&P 500 miss near the open fell from about 0.8–1.1% to about 0.05–0.25%, with other days unchanged. The logger's backfill can only rebuild dates whose contract Yahoo still serves (expired contracts disappear), so it skips older dates and keeps their existing rows.
