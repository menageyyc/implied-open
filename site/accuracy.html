What's being measured

The estimate. Exactly the main page's calculation (last close + the futures move since that close), saved every 5 minutes from 6 p.m. Eastern the evening before until 9:30 a.m.

Times are futures-price times. Yahoo's futures prices run about 10 minutes behind, so each estimate is filed under the time of the futures price it used, not the clock time. For example, the "9:20 a.m." estimate is what the page showed at about 9:30.

The actual open. When the market opens at 9:30, many stocks take a few seconds or minutes to make their first trade. The index's official opening number is worked out straight away, so for those stocks it still uses yesterday's closing prices, and it usually shows only part of the real move (for the Dow, often only about half). The index level at 9:31, after the first full minute of trading, is the first number that reflects nearly every stock, so it's the yardstick for the US indexes. The TSX gets going more slowly, so it's compared with its level at 9:35. You can switch between the 9:31 level, the 9:35 level and the official open with "Compare with". Yahoo keeps minute-by-minute prices for only about a month, so days before Oct 6, 2026 have no 9:31 level and use 9:35 instead (marked "9:35" in the table).

The "no change" guess assumes the index opens exactly at the previous close. An estimate is only useful if it beats that.

Rebuilt history. Days before Nov 4, 2026 were rebuilt from Yahoo's 5-minute futures history using the same formula (marked "rebuilt"). From Nov 4, 2026 on, the estimates are saved live as the page would show them.

Futures contract-switch days. Each quarter (March, June, September, December) the futures roll to the next contract, which trades at a different price. Until November 2026, Yahoo's data mixed the two contracts for a few days around the switch and the estimate could be off by close to 1%. The page and this tracker now use one specific contract throughout, and the September 2026 days were rebuilt that way, so these days (Tuesday of expiry week through the following Monday) are counted like any other. They still show in grey on the day-by-day chart, and you can untick the box to leave them out.

Holidays have no open, so they're skipped. The page and this tracker know the US and Canadian market holidays, so on the evening before a holiday the estimate is for the next real open. Source: Yahoo Finance.
