How it works

What Implied Open does, and how close it has come.

← Back to Implied Open

What it does

US stocks stop trading at 4 p.m. Eastern, but futures on the major indexes trade almost around the clock. Whatever happens overnight (Asia and Europe, after-hours earnings, the 8:30 a.m. economic reports) shows up in futures long before the 9:30 open. Implied Open turns that overnight futures move into an expected opening level for the S&P 500, Nasdaq-100, Dow, Russell 2000, Nasdaq Composite and Toronto's TSX.

How the estimate is made

  • Implied open = last close + how far the futures have moved since that close. Futures used: E-mini S&P 500, E-mini Nasdaq-100, Mini Dow and E-mini Russell 2000.
  • The Nasdaq Composite and the TSX have no free futures feed, so they borrow the Nasdaq-100 and S&P 500 moves. Treat those two as rougher.
  • Quarterly contract rolls, market holidays and 1 p.m. early closes are handled automatically.
  • Prices come from Yahoo Finance; futures run about 10 minutes behind. The page refreshes every minute.

After the bell

From 9:30 the page shows where each index actually opened: its level at 9:31, after the first full minute of trading. The "official" first print comes out at 9:30:00, before many stocks have traded, so it still counts them at yesterday's prices and lags the real move. On the Dow it typically shows only about half of the overnight gap.

An example: the morning after the 2026 midterms

  • At 1:30 a.m., the S&P 500 was implied 0.3% lower. By 5:30 a.m. it was implied 1.0% higher.
  • At 9:20 the page said 7,661 (+1.2%). At 9:31 the index stood at 7,659.
  • The first print showed only 7,636 (+0.9%). The Dow's first print said +1.2%; a minute later it was +1.9%.

The rest of the page

The overnight context strip (VIX, US 10-year yield, US dollar, Europe, Brent, copper) and the Canada list (oil, natural gas, gold, the loonie, Canada's 10-year, WCS) show what else has moved since the close. They help explain the futures move but aren't part of the estimate.
