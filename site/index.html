How to read this

What "implied open" means. The stock market is closed overnight, but futures on the S&P 500, Nasdaq-100 and Dow trade almost around the clock. However far a futures contract has moved since the market closed at 4 p.m. Eastern, the index is expected to open roughly that far up or down. Implied open = last close + that futures move.

It gets more accurate closer to the open. Overnight trading is light, so prices drift and can swing on little news. More traders join from about 8 a.m. Eastern, and economic reports often come out at 8:30 a.m. An estimate taken a few minutes before 9:30 is usually much closer to the real open than one taken the night before.

Limits. It's an estimate, not a forecast or advice. The first printed index level can differ a little, because not every stock starts trading at exactly 9:30. Prices come from Yahoo Finance's free feed, where futures prices run about 10 minutes behind and which isn't officially supported. Market holidays aren't accounted for. Futures used: E-mini S&P 500 (ES), E-mini Nasdaq-100 (NQ), Mini Dow (YM). The Nasdaq Composite has no futures of its own, so it borrows the NQ percentage move. The page refreshes itself every minute.
